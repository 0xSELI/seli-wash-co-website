Before you book

Questions we get most.

Do I need to be home?

No, as long as we can reach the surface and you have approved the estimate. We send a message when we arrive and when we finish.

Do you need my water and power?

Yes. We run off your outdoor water spigot and a working outdoor GFCI-protected outlet, and both have to be reachable from the area being cleaned.

How far can you reach from the spigot and outlet?

We carry 25 feet of pressure hose and a 25-foot extension cord. If the area you want cleaned sits further than that from an outdoor spigot and outlet, tell us when you ask — some jobs we can work around, and some we will have to turn down. We would rather sort that out by text than turn up and find we cannot reach.

Is an electric washer strong enough for concrete?

For the flatwork we take on — driveways, sidewalks, patios, porches, and steps — yes. It is also quieter than a gas machine and puts no engine exhaust next to your house while it runs.

What surfaces do you not clean?

We take on ordinary concrete flatwork. We do not offer roof washing, house or siding soft washing, gutters, fences, decks, sealing, or joint-sand replacement, and we do not work on decorative, painted, coated, sealed, stamped, or otherwise fragile surfaces. Anything unusual gets looked at first and may be declined — we would rather turn a job down than damage a finish.

What changes the price?

Size, buildup and staining, access, surface condition, any specialty treatment, and travel outside Duncan. There is a $50 minimum on standalone appointments, and the listed prices are introductory starting points rather than quotes.

When can you come out?

Jobs run Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9am to 6pm. Weekends are sometimes possible by request but are not guaranteed. Phone and text are answered 9am to 11pm. Every request is confirmed by hand, so treat the request as the start of a conversation rather than a booked slot.

Notes on this direction

The signature

The wash front is the brand device. It is ragged and wet-lit, not a straight UI divider, and it echoes as the amber hairline between sections and across service cards on hover. It is the one bold thing; everything else stays quiet.

three.js intent

The hero is the two supplied photographs, used as they came — nothing drawn on them, nothing generated, cropped by `cover` rather than stretched. In the built site the pair becomes a WebGL plane whose front dissolves under the same drag handle: one scene, one pass, paused off-screen. What you see here is that interaction prototyped in CSS so it can be judged before any 3D work starts.

Mobile

Below 820px this is a different composition, not a squeezed one: the reveal becomes a panel at the top and the copy moves below it onto solid navy, because text over a half-lit surface stops being readable at phone widths. The displacement filter and heavy glow are dropped, the drag target shrinks so it cannot swallow vertical scrolling, and call and estimate dock to the bottom of the screen. On the real site the WebGL layer is replaced entirely by a crossfade between two photos.

Reduced motion

All transitions and the load sequence stop; the reveal snaps rather than animates and stays fully operable by keyboard through a native range control. The built version adds a visible Before / After toggle so the comparison never depends on dragging.

Performance

No web fonts and no libraries; the only weight is the two concrete images, resized to 1500px and inlined so this file stands alone. A production build would serve them as WebP at several sizes and lazy-load the pair below the fold — and the headline and estimate button still render before either image is fetched.

Deliberately absent

There is no insurance card, no licence or certification claim, no guarantee, no rating, no testimonial, no job count and no years-in-business figure — because none of those exist yet. The gaps are the honest state of a business that has not taken its first customer. Nothing here should be filled in with marketing language later; it should be filled in with facts, or left out.

Still needed

Logo, real before-and-after photography, an owner photo and story, a Google Business Profile, and the Privacy and Terms pages. Domain, hosting, and form handling are unconfirmed. Response-time, lead-time, cancellation, weather, deposit and discount policies are all still open, so the site deliberately promises none of them.
